Routing Formulations ​

Steady flow routing ​

Under steady flow (executeSteadyFlow, Routing.cpp:637), links are processed in topologically sorted order. For each conduit:

  1. The upstream node's inflow QinQ_{\mathrm{in}}Qin​ is gathered (limited by the maximum outflow).

  2. The per-barrel flow is q=Qin/barrelsq = Q_{\mathrm{in}}/\mathrm{barrels}q=Qin​/barrels minus the conduit loss rate, clamped at zero.

  3. If q≥Qfullq \ge Q_{\mathrm{full}}q≥Qfull​: q=Qfullq = Q_{\mathrm{full}}q=Qfull​ and A=AfullA = A_{\mathrm{full}}A=Afull​; otherwise the normal-depth area is found from the inverse section factor,

    s=qβ,A=A(s),s = \frac{q}{\beta}, \qquad A = A(s), s=βq​,A=A(s),

    and the depth from y=y(A)y = y(A)y=y(A).

  4. The flow is uniform along the conduit (Qout=q⋅barrelsQ_{\mathrm{out}} = q\cdot\mathrm{barrels}Qout​=q⋅barrels, A1=A2=AA_1 = A_2 = AA1​=A2​=A); the link volume is V=A L barrelsV = A\,L\,\mathrm{barrels}V=ALbarrels.

Non-conduit links simply pass the upstream node's inflow through unchanged. Steady flow converges in one pass.

Kinematic wave routing ​

Governing equations ​

The kinematic wave model combines the continuity equation with the uniform-flow rating curve, dropping the inertia, pressure-gradient, and (partially) convective terms of the St. Venant momentum equation. It cannot represent backwater, flow reversal, or pressurization, and requires a directed acyclic network. Starting from the St. Venant equations and setting the bed slope equal to the friction slope, S0=SfS_0 = S_fS0​=Sf​, the governing pair is:

∂A∂t+∂Q∂x=0(continuity),\frac{\partial A}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} = 0 \qquad\text{(continuity),} ∂t∂A​+∂x∂Q​=0(continuity),

Q=β Ψ(A),Ψ(A)=A R2/3,β=1.486nS0(rating curve).Q = \beta\,\Psi(A), \qquad \Psi(A) = A\,R^{2/3}, \qquad \beta = \frac{1.486}{n}\sqrt{S_0} \qquad\text{(rating curve).} Q=βΨ(A),Ψ(A)=AR2/3,β=n1.486​S0​​(rating curve).

Finite-difference scheme ​

A weighted Wendroff implicit finite-difference scheme discretizes the continuity equation between the upstream (1) and downstream (2) ends:

with θ=φ=0.6\theta = \varphi = 0.6θ=φ=0.6. Because each junction has at most one outlet conduit, processing links in topological order leaves only A2t+1A_2^{t+1}A2t+1​ and Q2t+1Q_2^{t+1}Q2t+1​ as unknowns; Q1t+1Q_1^{t+1}Q1t+1​ is known from the upstream node's inflow and A1t+1A_1^{t+1}A1t+1​ from the inverse section factor at Q1t+1/βQ_1^{t+1}/\betaQ1t+1​/β. Root finding ​

Equation f(A2t+1)=0f(A_2^{t+1}) = 0f(A2t+1​)=0 is solved by a bracketed Newton–Raphson iteration (maximum 40 iterations). Because the section factor can have two roots (peak at Amax⁡A_{\max}Amax​ below full), the bracket is pre-screened: the initial bracket is [Amax⁡,Afull][A_{\max}, A_{\mathrm{full}}][Amax​,Afull​]; if both bounds produce the same sign it is reset to [0,Amax⁡][0, A_{\max}][0,Amax​]. If both bounds give negative fff the conduit is full (Q=QfullQ = Q_{\mathrm{full}}Q=Qfull​); if both give positive fff the flow is zero.

Storage nodes under kinematic wave ​

Storage nodes may have any number of outlet links. Their mass balance is integrated with the trapezoidal rule,

Vt+1=Vt+12(Qint+Qint+1−Qoutt−Qoutt+1)Δt,V^{t+1} = V^{t} + \tfrac{1}{2}\left(Q_{\mathrm{in}}^{t} + Q_{\mathrm{in}}^{t+1} - Q_{\mathrm{out}}^{t} - Q_{\mathrm{out}}^{t+1}\right)\Delta t, Vt+1=Vt+21​(Qint​+Qint+1​−Qoutt​−Qoutt+1​)Δt,

solved by successive approximation (ω=0.55\omega = 0.55ω=0.55, convergence tolerance 0.005 ft) because both QoutQ_{\mathrm{out}}Qout​ and VVV depend on the head. The storage head is then updated once more after all link flows are known (Routing.cpp:345–389).
