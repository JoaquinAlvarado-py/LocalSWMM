The 2D module and the 1D–2D coupling ​

The most far-reaching extension is the 2D surface runoff module: an unstructured triangular mesh on which the shallow water equations are solved in their local-inertial form (de Almeida & Bates, 2013):

∂h∂t+∇⋅q=i−e+s,∂q∂t+g h ∇η+g n2 ∣q∣ qh7/3=0,\frac{\partial h}{\partial t} + \nabla\cdot \mathbf{q} = i - e + s, \qquad \frac{\partial \mathbf{q}}{\partial t} + g\,h\,\nabla\eta + g\,n^{2}\,\frac{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\,\mathbf{q}}{h^{7/3}} = 0, ∂t∂h​+∇⋅q=i−e+s,∂t∂q​+gh∇η+gn2h7/3∣q∣q​=0,

where hhh is the water depth, q\mathbf{q}q the specific discharge (m2^22/s), η\etaη the free-surface elevation, nnn the Manning roughness, iii the rain over the mesh and eee, sss the evaporation and source terms. The term ∂q/∂t\partial \mathbf{q}/\partial t∂q/∂t (local inertia) is retained but the convective acceleration is omitted: accurate for subcritical flow of mild slope (Fr≲0.5\mathrm{Fr} \lesssim 0.5Fr≲0.5). The module is activated by the mere presence of a mesh ([2D_VERTICES] / [2D_TRIANGLES] or mesh file), and its solver is ported to GPU/WebGPU with bit-for-bit exactness with respect to the serial backend.

The coupling orifice law ​

The exchange between a 1D node and a 2D cell is an orifice-type discharge law governed by the head difference Δh=h2D−h1D\Delta h = h_{2D} - h_{1D}Δh=h2D​−h1D​ (NodeCoupling.cpp):

Q=Cd Aeff sign(Δh) 2g φ(∣Δh∣),Q = C_d\,A_{\mathrm{eff}}\,\mathrm{sign}(\Delta h)\,\sqrt{2g}\, \varphi(\lvert\Delta h\rvert), Q=Cd​Aeff​sign(Δh)2g​φ(∣Δh∣),

positive in the 2D→1D sense (drainage). The square root is regularized with the C1C^1C1 function

φ(x)={32ε x−12ε3/2 x2x<ε,xx≥ε,ε=0.02 m,\varphi(x) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{3}{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\,x - \dfrac{1}{2\varepsilon^{3/2}}\,x^{2} & x < \varepsilon,\\[1em] \sqrt{x} & x \ge \varepsilon, \end{cases} \qquad \varepsilon = 0.02\ \mathrm{m}, φ(x)=⎩⎨⎧​2ε​3​x−2ε3/21​x2x​​x<ε,x≥ε,​ε=0.02 m,

which matches x\sqrt{x}x​ in value and slope at ε\varepsilonε and has finite slope at the origin. This removes the singularity dQ/dΔh→∞dQ/d\Delta h \to \inftydQ/dΔh→∞ as Δh→0\Delta h \to 0Δh→0, exactly the filling/emptying equilibrium regime where the bare orifice was a source of stiffness and oscillations.

Three regularizations are additionally applied:

  • Lid-covered conduit gate: above the crown, a Hermite smoothstep over a 5 cm band multiplies QQQ; below the crown the exchange is exactly zero (the node only exchanges once it exceeds the crown). The effective area grows linearly from AAA at the crown to 2A2A2A at 5 cm above it.
  • Dry/wet ramp on the source side: QQQ is multiplied by a smoothstep over the live depth of the source side relative to the DRY_DEPTH, so that a drainage self-limits to zero when the cell empties and an overflow self-limits when the node empties.
  • Head sensitivity (Jacobian): the derivative −dQ/dh1D-dQ/dh_{1D}−dQ/dh1D​ is spread into the continuity denominator of the dynamic wave node (∑dQ/dH\sum dQ/dH∑dQ/dH), turning the explicit source into a damper that stabilizes the drainage/overflow iteration.

Volume budgets and cadence ​

The exchange is evaluated live inside the 2D marching scheme at level-0 cadence of the local time step, against current 2D heads and 1D heads frozen at the start of the batch, accumulating ∫Q dt\int Q\,dt∫Qdt exactly per point. Two hard caps guarantee that a drainage cannot take more than a fraction β\betaβ of the cell per substep and that the same water cannot overflow twice within a routing step. The exchange volumes of the chambers are delivered to the 1D through a delivery queue that drains at uniform rate over the batch span (not as a volume/Δt\mathrm{volume}/\Delta tvolume/Δt pulse that flooded small chambers and caused oscillations).

Coupling by ponding area ​

So that the HGL of a coupled node can rise above the crown and track the 2D surface (and so that overflow/inflow triggers), the 2D router overrides the ponding area of the node with the footprint of the surrounding 2D cells (the median dual area of the stencil cells), and marks the node as capable of ponding independently of the global ALLOW_PONDING option. This is the nodeCanPond of the dynamic wave extended to coupled nodes: the ponding area is not user data but the assigned 2D cell area.
